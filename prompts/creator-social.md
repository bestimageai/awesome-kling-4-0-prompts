## K019 — One pocket, three essentials

**Use:** everyday-carry creator clip. **Mode:** text to video. **Brief:** 12 seconds, 9:16. **Inputs:** none.

```text
An adult creator sits at a plain desk, framed from chest to tabletop in a fixed phone-camera view. They say, "This is what I actually carry." They place one key, one folded cloth, and one small card case on the desk in that order, each from the same jacket pocket. They look down at the three objects, then back at the camera with a brief smile. Daylight enters from the left; skin and fabric retain natural texture. Hear each object contact the table and quiet room tone. Keep all three objects visible at the end. No product claims, subtitles, or background music.
```

**Adjust:** personal items, jacket. **Review:** objects emerge from the pocket; count remains three; lip movement matches the line.

## K020 — The plant's good side

**Use:** a gentle visual joke. **Mode:** text to video. **Brief:** 10 seconds, 9:16. **Inputs:** none.

```text
An adult plant owner holds a small potted fern at chest height beside a bright window. They turn it slowly clockwise, searching for the best angle, then stop when one long frond droops across their face. They freeze for a beat, peek around the frond, and say, "That is apparently the good side." Use a stationary phone-style medium shot. Keep the pot level and the fern rooted in the soil. Hear leaf movement and the single dryly delivered sentence. The room remains softly lit and still. No subtitles, extra plants appearing, or music.
```

**Adjust:** fern variety, pot color. **Review:** frond moves with the pot; expression follows the obstruction; dialogue fits after the pause.

## K021 — Desk reset in one move

**Use:** satisfying organization clip. **Mode:** image to video. **Brief:** 10 seconds, 9:16. **Inputs:** overhead first frame of a desk with a tray, pencil, eraser, and ruler.

```text
Animate the supplied overhead desk view. Two adult hands move the pencil, eraser, and ruler into the empty tray one at a time, keeping every transfer visible. The pencil goes along the left edge, the ruler along the right, and the eraser between them. Finish by sliding the filled tray upward a short distance and withdrawing both hands. Keep the camera locked and the desk texture stable. Hear three distinct soft contact sounds and a final tray slide. Objects retain their original shapes and sizes. No jump cuts, extra stationery, written captions, or music.
```

**Adjust:** tray material, arrangement. **Review:** each object has a continuous path; final count matches the first frame.

## K022 — The quiet opening

**Use:** local-shop social post. **Mode:** text to video. **Brief:** 12 seconds, 9:16. **Inputs:** none.

```text
Film an adult bookshop owner from inside the shop as they unlock the front door, open it, and place a small wooden doorstop at its base. The morning street becomes audible only as the door opens. They step back, face the camera, and say, "Come in. Take your time." Keep a gentle handheld view that includes the door, floor, and owner's face without crossing the doorway. Warm shelves frame the background with unreadable book spines. End on the open entrance. Sound: key turn, hinge, street ambience, then the line. No subtitles or background music.
```

**Adjust:** shop type, wardrobe. **Review:** doorstop is placed after opening; street sound follows the door; keys stay in the same hand.

## K023 — A tiny celebration

**Use:** an understated achievement clip. **Mode:** text to video. **Brief:** 8 seconds, 9:16. **Inputs:** none.

```text
One adult at a kitchen table finishes a small wooden jigsaw by placing the final plain piece. ## K024 — The bag that keeps rustling
```

**Use:** a sound-led vertical comedy sketch. **Mode:** text to video. **Brief:** 15 seconds, 9:16. **Inputs:** none.

**Directing idea:** The repeated attempt escalates through timing and a second person’s reaction, without relying on a louder sound effect or a new prop.

```text
SETUP
A fixed phone-camera medium shot of an adult recording a quiet snack review at a kitchen table. One small paper bag of crackers is held in both hands. A second adult is seated in the soft-focus background reading a book. Ordinary evening room light, natural skin texture, no polished studio look.

COMIC TIMING
0–3s: The foreground creator leans toward the camera and whispers in English, "A very quiet snack."
3–7s: They gently separate the bag's folded top. The first movement produces a crisp paper rustle. They stop instantly and glance toward the background reader, who has not moved.
7–11s: The creator tries a much smaller movement. The paper makes one shorter rustle. The reader slowly lowers the book and says, in a normal dry voice, "We can all hear it."
11–15s: The creator's hands freeze, then lower the bag onto the table. They look into the camera without speaking. Hold the embarrassed reaction for two seconds.

SOUND MIX AND CONTINUITY
The rustles synchronize with the two opening attempts, not with the reaction shots. Keep speech intelligible and the final silence free of music or canned laughter. Maintain one bag, the same book, and each person's screen position. The foreground mouth stays shut during the reply. No cuts, subtitles, cracker eating, exaggerated facial distortion, or extra dialogue.
```

**Adjust:** room setting and the reader’s reply. **Review:** two attempts produce two rustles; reader reacts after the second; the final pause remains long enough to register.

## K025 — The scarf color vote

**Use:** fashion audience-engagement clip. **Mode:** text to video. **Brief:** 12 seconds, 9:16. **Inputs:** none.

```text
An adult creator stands before a neutral wall holding one rust scarf in the left hand and one blue scarf in the right. They lift the rust scarf beside their face and say, "Warm?" After lowering it, they lift the blue scarf and say, "Or cool?" They finish holding both at shoulder height with a curious expression. Use a fixed portrait camera and soft frontal daylight so colors remain faithful. Keep both scarves present throughout, with natural folds and separate hands. Hear fabric and the two short questions. No outfit transformation, on-screen poll, subtitles, or music.
```

**Adjust:** scarf colors, background. **Review:** speaker and scarf timing agree; colors do not drift between lifts.

## K026 — A window-seat review

**Use:** travel creator atmosphere. **Mode:** text to video. **Brief:** 15 seconds, 9:16. **Inputs:** none.

```text
An adult traveler sits at a train window with a closed notebook on their lap. Begin on a view of green fields passing outside, then pan slowly inward to the traveler. They look from the landscape to the camera and say, "I planned to read. I kept looking outside." They smile and turn back toward the window. Keep the train interior fixed while the landscape moves consistently in one direction. Natural window light changes gently as trees pass. Sound: steady wheels, low carriage ambience, and clear speech. No identifiable railway branding, subtitles, or music.
```

**Adjust:** scenery, notebook color. **Review:** exterior motion maintains direction; notebook remains closed; reflection matches the traveler.

## K027 — The oversized receipt

**Use:** a surreal consumer-culture social hook. **Mode:** text to video. **Brief:** 12 seconds, 9:16. **Inputs:** none.

**Directing idea:** The wider shot reveals the cost of the joke. Continuity of the paper makes the impossible quantity feel physically connected to the original action.

```text
VISUAL PREMISE
A tiny toy checkout sits on a full-size kitchen counter. One adult right hand holds a single small button-shaped purchase beside it. A narrow blank paper strip emerges only from the checkout's front slot. Keep the scale contrast clear in a locked close view.

ESCALATION
0–2s: The hand places the small purchase beside the toy, then presses its only button once. Start the printer sound after that press.
2–6s: The strip feeds steadily across the counter and curls over the front edge under gravity. The hand withdraws; no one pulls the paper.
6–9s: Cut to a lower, wider angle showing the same strip reaching the floor and forming a loose pile. Keep its upper end continuously connected to the slot. The checkout and purchase remain at the same counter positions.
9–12s: A second hand enters from below with a wastepaper basket and places it under the falling strip. The original right hand lifts the tiny purchase into view above the growing pile. Finish on the disproportion, while the printer continues.

MATERIAL AND AUDIO
Thin matte paper bends into irregular folds; it does not become a rigid ribbon or multiply into several strips. Use a small printer motor, soft paper impacts, and room tone. No dialogue, music, readable receipt, labels, extra button press, or teleporting paper. Preserve the already-fed paper through the cut.
```

**Adjust:** checkout color and the small purchase. **Review:** one press starts the feed; paper remains connected through the cut; the pile grows from falling material.
